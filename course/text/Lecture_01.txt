READING A NUMBER


Numbers in a small group


  DESCRIPTIVE
  Describing what you have in your hands
  The group is closed and you know every member of it.
  No uncertainty is involved at all.




  INFERENTIAL
  Talking about what you do not have
  You hold a sample and want to say something about a
  bigger population. Uncertainty is unavoidable — and it
  can be measured.

 Number 1 — '60% chance of rain this afternoon'

WHAT DOES IT MEAN?
Not 60% of the city, not 60% of the
afternoon
It means: across all past days when the
atmosphere looked like today, it rained on about
60% of them.

  'wrong'
• The only test: take the 100 days they said 60%, and check whether it
  rained on about 60

Measurement error is a property of the method
A survey asks: 'what was your income last month?'




  STREET VENDOR
  Estimates. Income varies day to day and nothing was written down, so the answer is a recollection, not a record.



  HIGH EARNER
  May under-report, for reasons that have nothing to do with the survey. The error here runs in one predictable
  direction.


  SALARIED EMPLOYEE
  Answers precisely. Three respondents, three error sizes — and the resulting average cannot be more accurate than
  the question that produced it.



             The fix is not to distrust the data. It is to know which direction the error runs, and say so.
