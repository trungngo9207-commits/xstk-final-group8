Pillar A: Framing the Problem




 CLO1 — Data-to-Knowledge Chain: Locate statistics in the Data → Information → Knowledge chain; explain why a statistic is a compression of
 information and identify what details are lost.

 CLO2 — Sampling & Central Limit Theorem: Distinguish a population parameter from a sample statistic; master sampling distributions and the Central
 Limit Theorem (CLT) as the cornerstone of valid statistical inference.

 CLO3 — Method & Data Identification: Given a business problem with data, correctly identify the question type (description, comparison, association,
 or forecasting), the data structure, and the appropriate family of methods.
Pillar B: Reading & Judging Results




 CLO4 — Chart Selection & Visual Pitfalls: Select suitable charts by data type and question; detect non-linearity, outliers, and hidden groups, while
 recognizing misleading graphics.

 CLO5 — Statistical Significance & Inference: Interpret confidence intervals, p-values, and regression coefficients accurately (including what they do
 NOT imply); separate statistical from practical significance.

 CLO6 — Critical Evaluation of Quantitative Claims: Evaluate claims for correlation vs. causation traps, omitted variables, violated assumptions,
 selective reporting, and over-generalisation.
Pillar C: Working with Data


 CLO7 — Data Preparation:
 Prepare an analysis-ready dataset and describe it thoroughly in a standard empirical summary table.



 CLO8 — Applied Analytics in Excel & Python:
  Execute data visualization, interval estimation, hypothesis testing, and multiple regression; effectively communicate findings to a non-
 statistical business audience.
